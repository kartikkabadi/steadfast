How it works

Set it once. Then it holds.

01

Pick what to block.

Start with YouTube, Instagram, X, Reddit, TikTok or Netflix, add any of fourteen categories, or type any domain. Subdomains come with it. Add apps too — they close as soon as they start.

02

Choose how long.

Thirty minutes, a few hours, or a custom length up to 365 days. Name what the time is for if you like — it stays in view while the block runs.

03

Commit.

Review exactly what’s blocked and when it ends. Nothing starts until you press the button.

04

Make room.

Your purpose and unlock time stay front and centre. Close the window — the block keeps running.

No pause button.
On purpose.

Once a block starts, you can’t pause it, shorten it or end it early in the app. It keeps working even if you quit — a separate background service holds your blocks and schedules, not the window.

Blocked apps that are already open get ten seconds to save, then close. Later launches stop before the app’s own code runs.

Straight answers

What it is, and what it isn’t.

What Steadfast does

  • Blocks websites across browsers through your Mac’s own resolver, including subdomains.
  • Closes blocked apps at launch, matched by bundle identifier — renamed copies too.
  • Keeps blocks running after you close or quit the app.
  • Keeps everything local. No account, no analytics, no remote API.

What it doesn’t pretend to do

  • It’s a DNS blocker, not a packet filter. Encrypted DNS, a VPN’s DNS, cached addresses or direct IP connections can get around it.
  • It can’t stop your Mac’s administrator. It holds a choice you made; it doesn’t try to defeat you.
  • It doesn’t measure focus or promise productivity. That part is still yours.

Get Steadfast.

Free and open source. Paste this into Terminal.

$ curl -fsSL https://github.com/kartikkabadi/steadfast/releases/latest/download/install.sh | /bin/bash

The installer verifies the download, then asks for administrator access to install the background service. The release uses a self-signed certificate and isn’t notarized by Apple — read the installer first if you prefer.
